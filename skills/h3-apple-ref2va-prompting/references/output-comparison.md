# Comparing outputs and diagnosing quality changes

Use this guidance for an authorized comparison or output review. Keep specific
versions, weights, settings, timings and results in the project's evidence
records. A successful case can motivate a candidate; it does not establish a
universally better model, adapter, prompt or enhancement method.

## Identify what the comparison can answer

A comparison of complete releases measures their combined behavior. To attribute
a change to one component, hold the other effective conditions fixed or state
the remaining confounders. Matching prompt text and seed alone does not establish
equivalent execution: reference preprocessing, model weights, adapter identity
and strength, sampling schedule, numerical paths and postprocessing can differ.
Check the actual inputs and resolved recipe when a causal claim depends on them.

Do not improve a frozen prompt while claiming to isolate an engine or adapter
change. Record a revised prompt as another intervention. Reusing a saved control
can be appropriate when its inputs and execution are demonstrably compatible;
one matching replay only establishes equivalence for that checked case.

## Compare the intended action as well as the artifact

For temporal defects, inspect the full progression and consecutive frames around
the affected event. Compare the same elapsed times and, when timing differs, the
same event phases. Cleaner frames caused by omitted, reduced or delayed motion,
changed framing or an incomplete action do not by themselves demonstrate better
motion quality. Verify that the requested behavior and resulting state survive.

Distinguish persistent duplicate contours from motion blur, occlusion and intended
stylization using their evolution across frames. Inspect the delivery at its
intended size and use crops to resolve uncertainty. An enlarged crop or isolated
pose can locate a defect without establishing its impact during playback.

## Keep conclusions within the inspected scope

Report action fidelity, identity, temporal artifacts, speech, visible text and
speed separately when relevant to the brief. A gain in one does not settle the
others. State which sequences and intervals were reviewed and whether review was
blind; limit claims to those inputs, settings and review methods. Partial relief
is an improvement with a remaining defect, not elimination of the problem.

Byte or decoded media equality can validate preservation or a runtime change;
it cannot establish that the shared output fulfills the creative brief. Choose a
delivery candidate using the user's priorities, while retaining failures and
uncertainties in the evidence rather than retroactively treating them as passes.
