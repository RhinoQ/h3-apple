# Sequence continuity and delivery review

Apply these principles according to the brief and creative form. A conventional
causal story, montage, performance and experimental sequence need different
criteria. Intentional repetition, ambiguity, discontinuity and audience address
are valid creative choices; distinguish them from unintended output errors.

## Design and plan for the intended reading

Select views and identity references appropriate to the requested deliverable.
When multiple views are required, check consistency of identity, proportions,
appearance and distinguishing features, and identify invented details that the
source does not establish. Different views of one identity do not automatically
imply additional subjects in a generated shot.

Identify the information a viewer needs to understand the intended progression.
Track relevant subject, object, spatial and action states across shots. For an
event whose meaning depends on its number, order or consequence, make those
relationships clear. Distinguish a completed event from sustained or repeated
activity; allow adequate time for the intended movement and response. Describe
compatible action and sound, without imposing a fixed number of beats or a
universal story structure.

For communication or interaction, establish who or what each line or action
addresses. Assess the combined evidence from staging, gaze, body orientation,
framing, sound and response. Use compatible spatial relationships where the
scene depends on them. Neither front-facing composition nor looking away is
inherently wrong; judge whether it conveys the intended interaction.

## Review the actual sequence

Assess the assembled sequence, including reused material, against that intent.
Observe continuous motion when the available tools support perception; inspect
dense consecutive frames around uncertain actions, interactions and cuts.
Sparse stills can miss temporal errors. State limits of motion or audio review;
successful decoding, a player's end event or a transcript does not establish
that the performance has been perceived and understood.

Locate critical transitions in the actual output rather than assuming the
prompt's timestamps were followed. For actions that change a state, inspect the
state before, the transition and the resulting state, including the ending when
relevant. Check contact, release, occlusion or reappearance where they determine
object identity, count, possession or spatial relationships.

Evaluate speech, visible performance, captions and editing together. Check
speaker attribution and timing without treating every cut-spanning line or
caption as a defect; motivated overlaps and reaction shots can be appropriate.
Inspect the progression and resolution of a beat, not only isolated poses.

Use speech recognition as supporting evidence. Music, noise or silence can
produce spurious transcripts; homophones can obscure an otherwise matching line.
Keep expected wording separate from recognition, and do not treat recognition
conditioned on the target lines as independent confirmation. Inspect visible
text separately: a transcript mentioning subtitles does not prove they appear,
and correct captions do not prove correct pronunciation. Listening is needed to
assess voice and delivery; lip sync also requires temporal visual review. If the
tools cannot support that perception, leave those judgments unverified.

Record material discrepancies with the file/version, time range, intended
meaning and observed result. Keep technical validity, local visual quality and
acceptance of the requested content separate. A partial improvement does not
qualify unreviewed parts of a sequence. Do not describe a candidate with known
material failures as an accepted deliverable; make unresolved issues clear when
showing work in progress. Prefer the smallest effective correction within the
task's scope and budget, preserving suitable existing material.

## Preserve acceptance through revisions

Identify the source version and the accepted properties or unresolved issues
before editing or enhancement. A newer render or a successful local correction
does not establish approval of the full sequence. After changes, recheck the
resulting sequence, including unchanged sections whose meaning or timing may
be affected.

After restoration or enhancement, compare the edited region against the source
for identity, gaze, expression and motion where relevant, as well as clarity.
Sharper individual frames can still alter the intended performance or flicker
across time. Preserving audio and pixels outside the edited region establishes
preservation of those components, not fidelity inside it.

Keep experimental baselines distinct from delivery candidates. Acceptance is
specific to the inspected version and scope; it does not transfer automatically
between edits. These practices support detection and correction of failures,
not a guarantee that a model will satisfy the brief on its first attempt.
