# Continuity and review

Derive criteria from the brief. Stories, montages, performances and experimental
sequences need different judgments. Intentional repetition, ambiguity, cuts and
audience address are valid choices.

## Plan the sequence

For multiple views, check identity, proportions and distinguishing features;
identify details invented beyond the reference. Several views of one identity
do not imply several subjects.

Track subject, object, spatial and action states. Make essential event counts,
order and consequences readable. Distinguish completed events from sustained or
repeated activity, and allow time for motion and response. Keep sound compatible
with action without imposing a fixed beat count or story structure.

Establish the recipient of speech and actions through staging, gaze, orientation,
framing, sound and response. Judge whether the combination conveys the intended
interaction; front-facing composition or looking away is not inherently wrong.

## Review the output

Review the assembled sequence, including reused material. Watch continuous motion
where the tools support perception; inspect consecutive frames around uncertain
actions, interactions and cuts. State motion/audio review limits. Successful
decoding, a player's end event or a transcript does not prove perceived performance.

Find actual transitions rather than assuming prompt timestamps were followed.
Check before, during and after a state change, including the ending where relevant.
Inspect contact, release, occlusion and reappearance when they determine identity,
count, possession or location.

Assess speech, performance, captions and editing together. Check speaker and timing;
intentional overlaps and reaction shots may be appropriate. Review complete beats,
not isolated poses.

Treat speech recognition as supporting evidence. Music/noise can produce false
transcripts; homophones can obscure correct speech. Keep expected lines separate
from recognition: target-conditioned transcription is not independent confirmation.
Inspect visible text separately. Listening is required for voice/delivery; lip sync
also needs temporal visual review. Leave unsupported judgments unverified.

Record discrepancies with file/version, time range, intended meaning and observed
result. Separate technical validity, image quality and fulfillment of the brief.
Label material failures and unfinished candidates clearly. Prefer the smallest
effective correction within scope and budget, retaining suitable material.

## Recheck revisions

Identify the source version, accepted properties and unresolved issues before edits.
Recheck the assembled result afterward, including unchanged sections whose meaning
or timing may shift. Approval applies to the reviewed version and scope.

After enhancement, compare identity, gaze, expression, motion and clarity with the
source. Sharper frames can change performance or flicker. Preserved audio and
unmasked pixels do not establish fidelity within the edited region.

Keep baselines distinct from delivery candidates. These checks help detect and
correct failures; they cannot guarantee first-attempt success.
